# Scientific abstract

Low-budget decoder recalibration may degrade neural-to-behavior prediction, but descriptive error mechanisms need not predict adaptation harm prospectively. We integrated two prespecified studies of authentic LINK threshold-crossing recordings from one macaque, using one source date and three inspected development dates with whole-trial partitions. Across 369 calibration allocations, prediction dispersion fell from approximately 13–21% of five-trial decoder error to 2% at thirty trials; allocation, scaling and regularization effects varied by date. No prespecified mitigation passed its practical and consistency gate. Leave-one-session-out calibration-only risk models likewise failed promotion: the combined model yielded 1.094×10⁻⁵ flexion⁴ risk MSE, versus 7.202×10⁻⁶ for label-only moments. Source-trained six-dimensional PCA with affine autoregression improved one-step recorded-neural forecasting 42.36% versus persistence, but only 3.59% versus a training mean and 2.40% versus temporally permuted fitting, below the 5% practical gate. Conditional trial-block intervals do not establish unseen-session or animal uncertainty. Three later recordings passed native eligibility but remained unscored under the stopping rules. These reproducible negative controls distinguish statistical calibration contributors from practical adaptation reliability and physiological drift; no subject invariance, live BCI benefit or neural foundation-model generalization is established.
